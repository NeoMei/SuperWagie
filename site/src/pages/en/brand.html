Brand and mark

An ox that
does the work.

Bēn Mǎ, the Chinese name, is written 犇马: 犇 is three oxen side by side, running. It reads as a herd in motion, and as a joke at our own expense. The mark turns those words into a head: one horn thrust forward, one curled back, the mane pressed flat by motion, one tooth left showing at the muzzle. It reads less like a software icon than like the colleague at the next desk who says little and keeps pushing the work forward.

Three layers in the Chinese name

犇: three oxen running together The character is built from three 牛, so the picture is already a herd moving as one. Strength, patience and a shared direction come with it, which is what pulling a piece of work forward together looks like.

犇马: the running beast of burden Ox and horse is an odd pairing, and that is the interesting part: the ox bears weight, the horse covers ground. Together they are “carries the load and keeps the pace” — patient with detail, quick enough to keep up. Said positively, it is the momentum of something that will not stop.

超级牛马, “super beast of burden”: a joke we own 牛马 is Chinese internet slang for the person who quietly does the work. Calling it super is admitting the label and then pushing it one step further: if the work has to be done, do it fast and do it well. There is a grin in the name, and something serious underneath — take the repetitive, tedious, time-consuming part off your desk.

The hard part of office work is rarely the idea. It is reading through the material, revising the draft until it can be sent, running the code until the tests pass. That work is exactly what a tireless character is for. The tooth is there so the ox never looks too stern: it can carry weight, and it is glad to be doing it.

Why SuperWagie

Supersets the ambition rather than describing a feature. This is not an assistant that helps out around the edges; it is meant to finish the whole job.

Wagieis informal English for someone who works for a living — the same idea as 牛马 in Chinese. It is looser than worker or employee: a little self-deprecating, and entirely at ease with being the one who does the work.

Put together, the English says exactly what the Chinese says: super beast of burden. The two names are one statement in two languages, not two separate brands. Wagie also carries a sense of being quick and busy, and it is short enough to live in an app name.

Use the Chinese name in conversation and document titles, the English name for the package, command line and localised interface. They point at the same product; do not transliterate one into the other.

Parts of the mark

At different sizes

The mark has to hold up at the 16px of a browser tab and at the 512px of an app icon: small sizes read by silhouette, large sizes by expression.

Ground and contrast

The mark uses a single signal orange, #FD4D06. It works on light interfaces, dark interfaces and plain white. Do not add outlines, shadows or gradients.

Downloads

The mark is a bitmap (PNG, transparent background). Use the large size for app icons and presentations, the small sizes for interfaces. Do not stretch it, recolour it, or overlay other shapes.
